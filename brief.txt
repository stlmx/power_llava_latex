尽管这些算法在某种程度上解决了部分电力设备状态监测领域的问题，然而这些算法均为单任务算法，即需要针对某个具体的任务、场景甚至设备进行数据搜集、模型训练以及部署的完整流程，这导致了以下问题：1）大量小模型带来的开发与管理的混乱。首先，不同小模型之间往往相互独立，无法被统一地进行协调与调度；此外，新的任务无法利用已有的模型库，导致资源的浪费与维护困难；2）模型协作的高门槛与复杂性。首先，对于复杂任务往往需要设计相互依赖的工作流，必须准确了解不同模型的输入输出、使用条件等；其次，基于人工设置的工作链路往往灵活性交较差，从而难以规模化地灵活应用。

随着以GPT-4o、DeepSeek为代表的大模型凭借强大的通用理解以及推理能力，在多个场景取得一定应用，为电力设备智能运维任务提供了新的思路。然而，现有的大模型无法直接应用于电力设备运维场景。首先，通用场景的大模型缺乏专业的电力知识注入，且停留在简单的图文问答阶段，难以处理高频电压等电力专业数据；此外，现有的大模型在电力领域的幻觉严重，对于需要精确数值计算或遵循物理定律的场景下，大模型的推理往往会产生不符合领域事实的“幻觉”，其数学能力和物理一致性依然薄弱。

为了解决上述问题，本文提出一种面向复杂电力任务的多智能体系统，将多个高成熟、高精度的专业电力小模型封装为标准化的工具库；以一个强大的规划大模型作为系统的“大脑”，负责理解复杂、多模态的任务指令，并且根据任务的需求，自主地从工具库中选择合适的工具，并规划出一个最优的、分步骤的执行路径（a plan or a workflow）；在执行完规划后，大模型负责汇总所有中间结果，进行最终的逻辑推理和因果分析，并以自然语言的形式生成一份专业、可解释的诊断报告。
该方案有效地结合了大模型的认知规划与小模型的专有任务的能力，对于电力设备的智能运维具有重要意义。




The modern electric power system, a quintessential highly-coupled Cyber-Physical System (CPS), is fundamental to socioeconomic stability. The operation and maintenance (O&M) and diagnostic tasks within these systems are inherently multi-modal and multi-stage, posing significant challenges to the precision and efficiency of monitoring technologies.

In recent years, Artificial Intelligence (AI), particularly deep learning, has demonstrated considerable success in power system condition monitoring [1, 2]. For instance, Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs) have been applied for insulator defect detection, while Recurrent Neural Networks (RNNs) are used for load forecasting. Nevertheless, these approaches predominantly adhere to a task-specific monolithic model paradigm, whose inherent limitations precipitate two critical issues. 1) Fragmented Development and Orchestration. A plethora of heterogeneous, isolated models have been developed for specialized sub-tasks (e.g., partial discharge identification, harmonic analysis), creating "siloed capabilities." This fragmented ecosystem lacks a unified interface for scheduling and coordination, leading to significant resource redundancy and maintenance challenges when addressing new tasks. 2) Rigid, Expert-Driven Collaboration. Complex scenarios requiring multi-model analysis currently rely on domain experts to manually design fixed workflows. Such hard-coded collaborative pipelines not only demand profound expert knowledge but also suffer from poor flexibility and robustness, thus impeding the large-scale, extensible deployment of intelligent technologies.

The advent of Large Language Models (LLMs), such as GPT-4o [3] and DeepSeek [4], presents a new paradigm. With their powerful general-purpose semantic understanding and reasoning capabilities, LLMs could theoretically serve as a unified interface to simplify task orchestration. However, their direct application in professional engineering domains reveals fundamental bottlenecks. Firstly, a critical "Modality Gap" exists. While adept at processing text and images, the architecture of general-purpose LLMs is not natively designed for specialized engineering data, particularly for performing essential mathematical operations (e.g., Fourier or wavelet transforms) on high-frequency time-series signals like transient overvoltages. This constrains their analytical capacity to superficial Visual Question Answering (VQA). Secondly, these models suffer from a deficiency in "Physical Consistency and Numerical Precision." Their inherent probabilistic nature often leads to "hallucinations"—outputs that contradict established physical laws or lack numerical accuracy—when tasked with scenarios requiring precise calculations or adherence to first principles.

To bridge the chasm between the cognitive breadth of LLMs and the execution depth of specialized computational models, this paper introduces a Multi-Agent Collaborative Framework for complex power system tasks. The core philosophy of this framework is to decouple cognitive planning from specialized execution. Specifically, we encapsulate a suite of mature, high-precision domain-specific models (e.g., fault feature extractors, equipment aging estimators) and fundamental mathematical operators (e.g., Laplace and Fast Fourier Transforms) into a standardized toolset. Concurrently, a powerful LLM, enhanced with domain knowledge, serves as the system's Cognitive Core. This core is responsible for: 1) Task Decomposition and Understanding: Interpreting complex, multi-modal user instructions. 2) Autonomous Planning: Autonomously selecting appropriate tools from the toolset and generating a logically coherent, step-by-step workflow tailored to the task's objectives. 3) Synthesis and Inference: Upon workflow completion, integrating all intermediate results from the tools to perform global logical reasoning and causal attribution, ultimately generating a professional, detailed, and interpretable diagnostic report.

In summary, by architecting an intelligent ecosystem guided by an LLM and executed by a diverse toolset, our proposed framework effectively synergizes the planning capabilities of large models with the computational precision of specialized agents. This approach not only offers a systematic solution to the prevailing challenges of fragmentation and rigid collaboration in power AI applications but also charts a new course toward achieving a higher level of automation and intelligence in power system operations and maintenance.
